🌱 What Is a Mole?

🏠 Underground Architects

Moles are small mammals that spend almost their entire lives underground, digging extensive tunnel systems with powerful shovel-like front paws. A single mole can dig up to 18 feet of tunnel per hour!

👃 Super Senses

Moles have terrible eyesight — some species are essentially blind. Instead, they rely on an incredible sense of touch and smell, using sensitive whiskers and snouts packed with nerve endings called Eimer's organs.

🧥 Velvet Fur

A mole's fur has no natural direction, so it can move forward or backward through tight tunnels without ruffling — like velvet! This lets them reverse at top speed without ever turning around.

🫁 Oxygen Masters

Moles can tolerate low-oxygen, high-CO2 environments in their tunnels. Some species can even reuse exhaled air by re-breathing it, letting them "smell" underwater too.

🔬 Types of Moles

⭐

Star-Nosed Mole

Famous for its bizarre nose with 22 fleshy tentacles, used to feel out prey underwater and underground faster than the human eye can track. The fastest-eating mammal on Earth!

🌾

Eastern Mole

The most common mole in North America. Builds the raised dirt ridges you see snaking across lawns, hunting grubs and worms just below the surface.

🍂

European Mole

Builds deep permanent burrow systems and even stores paralyzed live earthworms in underground "larders" for later snacking — a mole pantry!

🌲

Townsend's Mole

The largest mole in North America, found along the Pacific coast. Builds huge mole hills and can tunnel through surprisingly compact soil.

🎉 Fun Mole Facts

  • 🏃 Moles can dig tunnels at speeds up to 13 feet per hour using rotated, shovel-shaped front paws.
  • 👍 Moles have an extra "thumb" (a sixth digit) on each front paw to help them dig more efficiently.
  • 🏊 Many moles are excellent swimmers and will cross streams and ponds while foraging.
  • 💪 A mole can lift roughly 32 times its own body weight — relatively stronger than most mammals.
  • 👶 Baby moles are called pups and leave the burrow on their own after about a month.
  • 🌍 There are around 40 known species of true moles living across North America, Europe, and Asia.
  • ⏳ Despite popping up in lawns, most moles live their whole lives without ever being seen above ground.
